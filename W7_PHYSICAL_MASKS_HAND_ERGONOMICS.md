# W7 — Physical Masks / Hand Ergonomics for 0-Force Continuous Touch

Research question: does a physical hole mask / key guard / finger-separator placed on a capacitive touch pad plausibly make it usable, and what measured evidence exists?

Rule followed: every number below was seen in the cited source. Otherwise marked NOT FOUND. No guessing.

Interpretation for hole mask: only quantified physical-guard result found is Berg/McCormack (accuracy up, speed down, p<.01, n=1, mechanical keyboard, 1986/1990). Touch-overlay work shows benefit vs bare touch for dials (Tory) and qualitative blind-user benefit (Touchplates n=9), but the closest anchored-hand touchscreen test (avionic, n=24) found NO bezel-anchor benefit. No published capacitive hole-mask finger-rest accuracy % was found.

## Verdict: is a physical hole mask likely to help a capacitive touch pad?

- Evidence FOR (weak, indirect): physical keyguards increased accuracy (p<.01) at cost of speed (p<.01) in one n=1 cerebral-palsy keyboard study (https://commons.und.edu/theses/7477/); software "invisible keyguard" cut overlap errors 50–75% / corrected 80% (https://doi.org/10.1145/638271.638275); passive overlays beat bare touch for dial acquisition (https://doi.org/10.1145/2512349.2512812); blind-user touchplates (n=9) judged low-cost/adaptable (https://doi.org/10.1145/2513383.2513442); vibration bracing helps (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.08.005). Plausible mechanism: walls stop slip, allow rest, reduce overlap/double-hits.
- Evidence AGAINST / caution (direct): only controlled anchored-hand touchscreen test found (avionic Fitts, n=24, 0.8–2 cm targets) found NO bezel-anchor benefit vs freehand, while vibration cost was 6.5→5.7 bps and 10.3%→16.6% errors (https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2023.2212225). Touch baselines are 28–39 WPM at 1.0–3.6% CER (https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702597) and 36.2 WPM / 2.3% (https://doi.org/10.1145/3338286.3340120) — far below steno 180–225 WPM thresholds (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenotype), and capacitive sensing cannot track through thick insulating walls without retuning. Guard also costs speed (Berg/McCormack) and gesture keyboards already cut errors 37–52% in software (https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702357).
- Missing: NO published capacitive hole-mask finger-rest accuracy %, NO hole pitch/diameter rule, NO N/mm² contact-pressure figure, NO touchscreen-steno WPM, NO 2024–2026 tablet handwriting absolute % were found and verified this session — all marked NOT FOUND above.
- Net: hole mask is plausible as a slip/rest aid for slow tap or braced selection, but unlikely to make a 0-force continuous surface steno-grade without new measurement: expect small accuracy gain + speed loss (per guard literature), not an order-of-magnitude jump to 200 WPM. Recommend building one mask variant and measuring substitution/deletion rate + WPM vs bare touch (report n, target size, throughput bps, CER) before claiming benefit.
