# W6 — Double-Ring 16-State Thumb Compass + Simultaneous Two-Thumb Chords: Viability Research

Claim under test: a "double ring" 16-state thumb compass (8 inner + 8 outer radial zones) and simultaneous two-thumb chords are physically and cognitively viable.

Rule: ONLY numbers seen in a source, with URL. "NOT FOUND" otherwise. No guesses.

## 1. Thumb radial / circumduction range of motion; discrete directions reliably indicable

### 1a. Measured thumb ROM (adults)

From Barakat et al., n=100 consecutive individuals without thumb pathology, right thumb, mean age 26 (range 18–37), 62 female / 38 male:
- IPJ flexion mean 88° (range 80–90°), extension mean 12° (range 0–45°) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653006/
- MCPJ flexion mean 60° (range 43–70°), extension mean 8.1° (range 0–15°) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653006/
- CMCJ palmar abduction (anteposition) mean 61.2±4.4° (range 50–71°) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653006/
- CMCJ retroposition mean 31±4° (range 25–38°); abstract reports retroposition as "31.1 mm (25–38 mm)" (unit inconsistency in source) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653006/
- CMCJ radial abduction mean 62.9±4.3° (range 53–71°) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653006/
- Opposition Kapandji grade mean 9±0.5 (grades 9–10) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653006/
- Adduction mean 10.2±4° (range 5–20°), with inter-observer coefficient 0.02 and intra-observer 0.04 (no reliability) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653006/
- Excluding adduction, mean inter-reliability 0.82 (p<0.01), mean intra-reliability 0.93 (p<0.01) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653006/

From 4D-CT circumduction pilot (1 healthy volunteer, 18 volumes over 2-s cycle; cadaver validation avg rotational error <1°):
- CMC flexion-extension 27.3°, adduction-abduction 66.9°, pronation-supination 10° during circumduction — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23690152
- Translation of estimated contact-area centre 4.1, 4.0, 1.0, 1.5 mm for key-pinch→adduction, adduction→palmar-abduction, palmar-abduction→opposition, opposition→key-pinch — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23690152

From thumb-rotation video jig, n=27 healthy volunteers:
- Rotation (abduction-extension to maximal circumduction) varied 70° to 110°; no gender or right/left differences — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14554232 (via search snippet for https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0736026603001141)

From in-vivo 3D CT, n=9 male Japanese volunteers, mean age 34.3 y (range 32–42 y), radial abduction → opposition via palmar abduction:
- First metacarpal abducted 26.6°±6.6° (p<0.05), internally rotated 22.3°±4.0° (p<0.05), flexed 25.0°±4.5° (p<0.05) — https://www.jhandsurg.org/article/S0363-5023%2816%2930833-4/fulltext

From pollexograph/goniometry reliability study (healthy adults):
- Pollexograph-thumb: radial adduction 12°, abduction 71°; all other angular methods ~20° radial adduction and ~50° radial abduction — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2000656X.2020.1828899
- Palmar abduction 47° to 57° for Pollexograph-metacarpal, radius-metacarpal, intermetacarpal-angle techniques — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2000656X.2020.1828899
- Intermetacarpal distance 50 mm / 60 mm (adduction/abduction); thumb-DIP distance 50 mm / 120 mm — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2000656X.2020.1828899
- All other angular methods range 20°–27° radial adduction, 51°–55° radial abduction — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/2000656X.2020.1828899

### 1b. How many discrete directions a thumb can reliably indicate on a plane

NOT FOUND: no study found that directly measures "how many discrete thumb circumduction directions on a plane can be reliably indicated" in degrees-per-zone or N-way thumb-joystick classification accuracy.

Closest proxies (hand/finger/stylus radial-direction selection, not thumb-circumduction-plane specifically):

- Kurtenbach et al. via M3 paper: "to keep the error rate under 10%, a menu with eight targets on a level can not go beyond the level of two" — https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/673bee63a30b0f320a61a2a7a1b2e797f6853ec0.pdf
- Expert hierarchic marking-menu limits (Kurtenbach 1993 summary): "For menus of four items, even up to four levels deep, the error rate was less than ten percent. This is also true for menus of eight items, up to a depth of two. However, when using markings for menus with eight items or more, at depths greater than two, selection becomes error-prone, even for the expert." — https://www.billbuxton.com/MMExpert.html
- Same source: "For either device [mouse/pen], error rates were below 10% for up to menus of breadth 8 and depth 2." — https://www.billbuxton.com/MMExpert.html
- Same source: "Subjects produced significantly more errors with the mouse than with the pen (F(1,11)=6.41, p<.05). Both depth and breadth interacted to affect error rate (F(6,66)=12.28, p<.001)." — https://www.billbuxton.com/MMExpert.html
- Pollock thesis summary of marking-menu literature: "within a single menu level, marking menus typically only support a maximum of 8 options to minimize error rates" — http://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/theses/thesis-jordanpollock-final.pdf
- Same thesis: "they only support up to 8 items per menu and two levels of depth to retain 90% selection accuracy" and "Marking menus can reliably hold up to only 8 menu items in a single menu level, or 64 items over two levels." — http://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/theses/thesis-jordanpollock-final.pdf
- Kurtenbach/Sellen/Buxton 1993 novice data (all menus equal diameter; 40 trials): Exposed mean RT 0.98 s (SD 0.23), 0.64 errors (SD 1.00), 1.6%; Hidden 1.10 s (SD 0.31), 3.27 errors (SD 3.57), 8.2%; Marking (ink trail) 1.10 s (SD 0.31), 3.76 errors (SD 3.67), 9.4%; interaction group×menu-size RT F(10,110)=3.5, p<.001; errors F(10,110)=64.7, p<.001 — https://www.billbuxton.com/PieMenus.html and https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/marking-menus-93.pdf
- Same study qualitative: "even-numbered menu sizes allowed subjects to easily find the target slice (sizes 4, 8, and 12)" — https://www.billbuxton.com/PieMenus.html
- Callahan et al. 1988 pilot (n=16, 8-item pie vs linear, mouse): "users were approximately 15% faster with the pie menus"; error-rate ANOVA "marginally statistically significant differences (P=0.087) between pie and linear menus" — https://www.cs.umd.edu/~ben/papers/Callahan1988empirical.pdf
- M3 Gesture Menu (finger, compound-stroke, touchscreen): Depth-1 {T:0.6 s, E:0.35%}@{D:1,C:8}; Depth-3 {T:1.0 s, E:8.85%}@{D:3,C:512}; Original Marking (Kurtenbach & Buxton stylus) Depth-2 7% / Depth-3 17% at 1.4 s / 2.3 s; Zhao & Balakrishnan stylus Depth-2 10% / Depth-3 17% at 2.3 s / 3.6 s; Multi-Stroke Marking Depth-2 4% / Depth-3 7% at 2.3 s / 3.4 s — https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/673bee63a30b0f320a61a2a7a1b2e797f6853ec0.pdf
- A-Coord thesis context (pen tilt menus): "a tilt menu with four or eight items had less errors than twelve menu items" (exact % NOT FOUND in accessible snippet) — https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/assets/publication_files/2012-MScThesis-khalad.pdf

NOT FOUND: measured degrees of thumb circumduction total sweep (360° cone angle) as a single number; thumb-isometric-joystick "N discrete directions at X% accuracy" study.

## 3. Bimanual simultaneous gesture entry; two-hand / bimanual chords; simultaneous vs sequential; WPM + error rates

### 3a. Simultaneous bimanual sequences are much harder than unimanual; little/no transfer

Yokoi et al., n=32 (21W/11M, age 23±5 y), 5-item discrete sequence production, force threshold 2.3 N, simultaneous = both fingers above/below threshold simultaneously, 4 days:
- Day-1 test MT: bimanual 6.7±1.30 s vs unimanual 2.8±0.57 s ("more than twice as long", "far more difficult") — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338615/
- After 4 days: bimanual 2.0±0.58 s vs unimanual 1.1±0.30 s — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338615/
- Day-1 proportional MT reduction: unimanual 54±15% vs bimanual 27±18% (F1,23=6.65, P=0.0005) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338615/
- No-cue group: no significant transfer unimanual→bimanual (F2,14=0.27, P=0.77), bimanual→unimanual (F1,7=0.11, P=0.75), within-bimanual (F2,14=1.89, P=0.19) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338615/
- Cue group (letter cues A–D left / 1–4 right): unimanual→bimanual F2,14=6.24, P=0.012; bimanual→unimanual F1,7=9.13, P=0.019; bimanual→bimanual F2,14=11.22, P=0.001; estimated transfer 11.5% / 9.3% / 25.7% of sequence-specific gains; cross-condition differences n.s. (t7=−1.88, P=0.10; t7=−1.895, P=0.10) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338615/
- Explicit recall: cued participants reproduced trained 63±26% correct vs untrained 33±33% (F1,14=36.80, P<0.0001) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338615/
- Task analysis note: unimanual = 5 locations→5 fingers; bimanual = 25 combos of locations → 25 finger combos (or sequential L/R selection) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338615/
- Reaching literature cited therein: ~60% unimanual↔bimanual transfer for force-field adaptation (Nozaki et al. 2006); ~50% for reaching (reviewed) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338615/

### 3b. Bimanual HCI: faster when coupled, not when truly simultaneous-independent

- Leganchuk/Zhai/Buxton positioning+scaling: "Overall, the bimanual techniques resulted in significantly faster performance than the status quo one-handed technique, and these benefits increased with the difficulty of mentally visualizing the task"; "no significant difference between the two bimanual techniques"; "For the smallest target that did not require much planning and visualization, two-handed was in fact inferior to one-handed (p<0.001 C1 vs S2) or was not statistically different (p 0.84 C1 vs T2)" — http://billbuxton.com/ToCHI2H.pdf
- Authors' motor-control review note: "simultaneous independent timing for the two hands could not be achieved even when parallel control was encouraged by training" — http://billbuxton.com/ToCHI2H.pdf
- A-Coord pen auxiliary channels (tilt/roll/pressure, n≈10–12): single-channel errors "range between 5% and 20%"; tested a-coord T+R 5.4% (s.e. 3.1%, p=0.220 n.s. vs others in that contrast); 8 levels 18.2% (s.e. 1.8%) vs 4 levels 9.4% (s.e. not in snippet for 4-level SE; "twice as many errors with 8 levels"); Technique F4,36=4.47, p=0.01; Levels F1,9=35, p<0.001; Distance F2,18=1.93, p<0.001 — https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/assets/publication_files/2012-CHI-khalad-acoord.pdf

### 3c. Chord / two-thumb WPM + error rates

- Twiddler one-handed chording experts (n=5): average 47 wpm after ~25 h practice — https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/1364695
- Twiddler vs multi-tap longitudinal novices: multi-tap max avg 20 wpm; Twiddler avg 47 wpm; one user 67 wpm — https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/fac/Thad.Starner/030_research.htm
- Mini-QWERTY two-thumb longitudinal (20×20-min sessions, 11 days): session-1 mean 31.72 wpm (SD 7.00); session-20 mean 60.03 wpm (SD 8.40); error (Soukoreff-MacKenzie total) session-1 6.12% (SD 3.46%) → session-20 8.32% (SD 4.13%); fastest single session 74.69 wpm — https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~thad/p/030_10_MTE/mini-qwerty-chi05.pdf
- Large-scale mobile typing (n=37,370, 160 countries): mean 36.2 wpm with 2.3% uncorrected errors; two-thumb 38 wpm vs one-finger 29 wpm; one participant 85 wpm; 74% typed with two thumbs — https://userinterfaces.aalto.fi/typing37k/resources/Mobile_typing_study.pdf and https://www.fox5ny.com/news/study-finds-people-can-now-type-on-smartphones-nearly-as-fast-as-on-computer-keyboards
- Same study: autocorrect +~9 wpm; word prediction −2 wpm; teens ~40 wpm vs 40s 29 wpm / 50s 26 wpm; 6 h/day device use — https://www.fox5ny.com/news/study-finds-people-can-now-type-on-smartphones-nearly-as-fast-as-on-computer-keyboards and https://www.theguardian.com/science/2019/oct/02/ready-text-go-typing-speeds-mobiles-rival-keyboard-users
- DigiTouch thumb-to-finger (two-thumb-like split layout, n=10, 10×20-min sessions): session-1 7.0 wpm (SD 2.2) → session-10 16.0 wpm (SD 4.1); overall mean 13.0 wpm (SD 4.12); last-session uncorrected error 0.85%; session-1 corrected error 25.0% (SD 11%) → session-10 15.8% (SD 10%) — https://ubicomplab.cs.washington.edu/pdfs/digitouch.pdf
- Chord-keyboard ergonomics review (citing Kroemer 1992; Gopher & Raij 1988): 59 chords memorized within ~3 h (n=10 university); after 35×1-h sessions chord 160 cpm vs QWERTY 105 cpm (n=15 no-typing-experience, 3 groups); chord learning percentage 77%; rated most cognitively (0.88) and physically (0.61) demanding vs QWERTY/split controls — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/216747/cdc_216747_DS1.pdf

NOT FOUND: "two-thumb chord" (simultaneous left-thumb-state + right-thumb-state chord) WPM or error rate as a distinct technique; simultaneous two-sided radial-gesture chords faster-than-sequential controlled comparison with % and p-value.

## 4. Cognitive limits on number of arbitrary gesture/state mappings; training effects; failure rates

- Chord memorization capacity: 59 chords for two-handed ternary 8-key board memorized "within about 3 hr" (Kroemer 1992, n=10) — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/216747/cdc_216747_DS1.pdf
- Chord speed after extended training: 160 cpm chord vs ~105 cpm QWERTY after 35 one-hour sessions (Gopher & Raij 1988, n=15 novices) — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/216747/cdc_216747_DS1.pdf
- Chord learning cost: 77% learning percentage; highest cognitive (0.88) + physical (0.61) demand ratings — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/216747/cdc_216747_DS1.pdf
- Marking-menu breadth×depth trade-off (expert, pen/mouse): 4-breadth to depth 4 <10% error; 8-breadth to depth 2 <10% error; ≥8-breadth beyond depth 2 "error-prone even for the expert"; 64 items via 4×4×4 ≈ 8×8 in speed+error — https://www.billbuxton.com/MMExpert.html
- M3 high-capacity finger gestures: 512 commands (8×8×8, depth 3) at 1.0 s, 8.85% error; 64 commands (depth 2) at 0.7 s, ~3% error (Table 2) vs original marking 7–10% (depth 2) / 17% (depth 3) — https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/673bee63a30b0f320a61a2a7a1b2e797f6853ec0.pdf
- Discretization penalty (A-Coord): 4 levels 9.4% vs 8 levels 18.2% errors ("twice as many errors with 8 levels") — https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/assets/publication_files/2012-CHI-khalad-acoord.pdf
- Thumb-micropose recognition (ThumbTrak, n=10, 12 phalanx poses): mean 93.6%; highest 97.50%, lowest 88.79%, median 94.10%; index-proximal 85.72% (confused with index-middle/middle-middle/middle-proximal) — https://czhang.org/assets/pdf/ThumbTrak.pdf
- Ring micro-gestures (Ring-a-Pose, n=10, 7 classes): 90.60% user-independent, 99.27% user-dependent; joint error 14.1 mm UI / 10.3 mm UD — https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12980v2
- Template-matcher floor ($P, best of tested): error 16.2% (accuracy 83.8%) for user-articulated gestures in that evaluation — https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/chi-17.pdf (via search snippet "Even $P, the best recognizer among those evaluated in this work, delivered an error rate of 16.2% (83.8% accuracy)")
- $N: 98% accurate with 8 training examples per symbol on MMG corpus (20 adults, 1–3 stroke symbols) — https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/c479/68fc7e28c7294ad0b44b28cf13347797cbfa.pdf

NOT FOUND: absolute "N arbitrary gestures learnable" number from motor-learning literature; Miller 7±2 or Hick's-law prediction applied to 16-state thumb compass with measured failure rate; training-hours→reliability curve for 16-state double-ring thumb compass specifically.

## Verdict: MARGINAL (not defensible as stated; not disproven — requires narrowing + data)

- Physically the thumb has the excursion (CMC radial abduction 62.9° [53–71°] + palmar abduction 61.2° [50–71°] + MCP 60° + IP 88°, Barakat — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653006/; CMC circumduction components 27.3°/66.9°/10° — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23690152/; rotation 70–110° — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14554232), so 8 radial directions (45° sectors) fit inside the available cone on paper. But reliable human radial selection caps at 8 per level for <10% error (4-deep only if breadth=4; 8-breadth fails beyond depth 2 — https://www.billbuxton.com/MMExpert.html; "max 8 to minimize errors / 90% over 2 levels" — http://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/theses/thesis-jordanpollock-final.pdf; 8-targets/level cannot exceed depth 2 at <10% — https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/673bee63a30b0f320a61a2a7a1b2e797f6853ec0.pdf). A second concentric ring doubles the decision from 8 to 16 (direction + radius) with no thumb-specific accuracy number found; the closest discretization penalty doubles errors (9.4% at 4 levels → 18.2% at 8 levels — https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/assets/publication_files/2012-CHI-khalad-acoord.pdf), and thumb tapping needs ≥9.2–9.6 mm targets to avoid error inflation (Parhi — https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2006/01/parhi-mobileHCI06.pdf) with thumb spread We≈3.8–4.0 mm (CDC — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/224404/cdc_224404_DS1.pdf). 16 radial+radii zones per thumb are therefore MARGINAL without a measured thumb-confusion matrix.
- Simultaneity is the harder problem: simultaneous bimanual 5-press sequences take 6.7±1.30 s vs 2.8±0.57 s unimanual pre-training and remain 2.0±0.58 s vs 1.1±0.30 s after 4 days, with zero transfer without explicit cues and only ~10% (11.5%/9.3%) with cues (25.7% within-bimanual) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5338615/. HCI bimanual gains come from coupled/asymmetric roles, not independent simultaneous selection (Leganchuk — http://billbuxton.com/ToCHI2H.pdf; "could not achieve simultaneous independent timing even with training" — http://billbuxton.com/ToCHI2H.pdf). No simultaneous two-thumb-chord WPM/error found (NOT FOUND above), while sequential two-thumb typing already does 38 wpm at 2.3% uncorrected (37k study — https://userinterfaces.aalto.fi/typing37k/resources/Mobile_typing_study.pdf) and chording needs ~25 h to 47 wpm (Twiddler — https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/1364695) or 20 sessions to 60.03 wpm at 8.32% error (mini-QWERTY — https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~thad/p/030_10_MTE/mini-qwerty-chi05.pdf). Claiming simultaneous chords beat sequential is NOT defensible on current numbers.
- Cognitively 16 states per thumb (256 combined) exceeds demonstrated reliable vocabularies at low error: best finger compound-stroke case is 512 at 8.85% / 1.0 s and 64 at ~3% / 0.7 s (M3 — https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/673bee63a30b0f320a61a2a7a1b2e797f6853ec0.pdf); 59 chords need ~3 h just to memorize with 77% learning and highest demand ratings (CDC review — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/216747/cdc_216747_DS1.pdf). Thumb-ring sensing of 12 poses already drops to 93.6% mean (85.72% worst cell — https://czhang.org/assets/pdf/ThumbTrak.pdf).
- Bottom line: 8-direction single-ring per thumb is grounded (8 at <10% to depth 2); 8+8 double-ring per thumb and simultaneous bimanual chords are MARGINAL — viable only if (a) inner/outer radius threshold is proven ≥ thumb We + target-size margin, (b) 16-way thumb confusion is measured <~10%, and (c) simultaneous-vs-sequential is A/B-tested for time+error, since today (a)–(c) are NOT FOUND and the nearest numbers predict error-doubling and >2× bimanual difficulty.

DONE
