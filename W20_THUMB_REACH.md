## Verdict

- r=30mm is REACHABLE as a maximum excursion: group-mean max reaches (51.8-61.4mm) exceed 30mm, free-thumb circumduction path (~226mm DERIVED) exceeds a 30mm full-circle perimeter (~188.5mm DERIVED), and one cited two-hand tablet comfort radius (38mm) exceeds 30mm.
- r=30mm is NOT established as COMFORTABLE/sustainable in the tested postures: the 90%-consensus comfort patch is ~4x10mm and the 70%-consensus box is ~22x30mm (Otten), so a 60mm-diameter sweep exceeds the directly measured comfort envelope; the 38mm comfort radius is a single secondary citation for an edge-held tablet, not a flat six-finger-anchored pad, and must not carry the decision alone.
- Largest radius the evidence directly supports: ~11-15mm half-width from the 70% consensus box (30.4mm wide -> ~15mm radius; 21.9mm tall -> ~11mm radius), i.e. r≈12-15mm comfort-supported, r≈20mm as theWorking upper bound only if accepting the 70% (not 90%) envelope plus max-reach margins; r=30mm exceeds all direct comfort measurements.
- Decision: do NOT lock r=30mm as comfortable on 224x148 two-handed anchored use. Evidence supports designing at r≈15mm (defensible) to r≈20-22mm (tolerant, needs validation); r=30mm should be treated as max-reach, requiring your own anchored-posture comfort/fatigue study before adoption. The accuracy gain 82%->96% (20->30mm) is therefore in direct tension with comfort evidence, and the return-leg time cost argument favors the smaller end.
- Geometry note: on 224x148, a 30mm arc fits on paper (half-width per hand 112mm), so geometry is not the blocker — human comfort data is.
