## Conclusion: best map onto thumb-compass + LM

- Recommended: SHARK2-style continuous-trajectory + language-model decoding combined with $P/$Q-style point-cloud template matching, with n-gram-driven layout assignment from GA / simulated-annealing literature, and steno-style syllable packing only as dictionary briefs.
- Why, with cited numbers:
  - Only continuous gesture + LM path has a sourced mobile-word rate directly comparable to thumb input: Swype predicted over 50 words per minute with CTO at 55 words per minute and Guinness 25-word message in 25.94 seconds at nearly 58 words per minute / 370 characters per minute — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swype — versus letter-chord novice rates near 15.2 words per minute mean / 19.2 words per minute max after 250 minutes and ~20 words per minute after ~350 minutes — https://infoscience.epfl.ch/handle/20.500.14299/96130 — and versus Twiddler 47 words per minute after ~25 hr — https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327051hci2104_1.
  - Only $-family gives a sourced lightweight on-device vector matcher claim suitable for a compass: $Q achieves whopping 142× speedup over $P while slightly improving accuracy — https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/qdollar.html — and $1 needs very few templates with only about 100 lines of code — https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/ — fitting a small directional alphabet where stroke number/order/direction invariance ($P) matches thumb-vector noise.
  - Layout automation to exploit this is sourced: GA minimizing weighted bigram distance yields average 6.04% travel reduction over QWERTY across 40 texts — https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0226611 — and Engram-style bigram-frequency search over 100 million layouts — https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202103.0287/v1/download — directly transfers to assigning frequent syllables to the most separable compass directions.
  - Syllable packing is kept as second-order because professional steno ceilings (180, 200, 225 words per minute required; up to 375 words per minute; many at 300 words per minute after three years — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenotype — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorded_keyboard) and Velotype 200 words per minute syllabic rate — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velotype — require 3.5 to 4 chords per second professional discipline or 6.25 to 10.83 chords per second if forced per-syllable in fast languages — https://plover.readthedocs.io/en/latest/system-dev/key-arrangement.html — which a thumb compass cannot sustain; LM-corrected vectors avoid that motor burden.
  - Dasher-style continuous LM zoom is the conceptual analogue but has WPM/error: NOT FOUND in fetched pages — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasher_(software) — so it cannot be the primary cited-speed basis; use its idea (probabilistic enlargement of likely continuations) inside the Swype/$Q decoder rather than as layout.
- Explicit gaps: seq2seq gesture-typing accuracy/latency: NOT FOUND; $N/$P absolute accuracy %: NOT FOUND; multitouch chord-vs-sequence accuracy and steno segmentation accuracy: NOT FOUND.
