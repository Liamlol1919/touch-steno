# W12 — DRIFT and ENSLAVEMENT/Finger-Coupling Prior Art

Scope: check whether two claimed solved failure modes were solved first.
Our claims under test:
- Mode 1 DRIFT: resting finger drifts 11.5 mm over 20 s; adaptive baseline (sliding 200 ms window) reduces feature noise to 5 mm.
- Mode 2 ENSLAVEMENT: neighbouring fingers move 62–92% in parallel with a leader; per-pair linear regression suppresses it.

Rule followed below: every numeric value has a source URL. Where no source gives a number, marked NOT FOUND. Nothing guessed.

## Verdicts (blunt)

### Mode 1 — DRIFT: partly known problem, apparently new specific measurement + fix
- KNOWN: capacitive baseline/drift compensation (slew-rate reference followers, temp/humidity drift in counts/°C, forced re-baseline after long touch) is textbook and patented. Systematic touch OFFSET (~4 mm → 1.6 mm via Holz & Baudisch) and tap-accuracy regression (Weir ~23%/14%/5%) are published. Slow hand-drift across repeated taps with reference-point tracking (Findlater/Wobbrock) is published as a concept.
- NOT FOUND: any published resting-finger centroid drift of 11.5 mm over 20 s, any 200 ms sliding-window adaptive baseline applied to resting-touch XY, or any 11.5 → 5 mm noise-reduction number for that case. Industry "touch-point drift" pages give no mm. Tremor literature gives target sizes, not resting drift kinetics.
- Bottom line: you did NOT invent drift compensation. You MAY have the first quantified resting-touch 20-s drift magnitude + 200-ms-window fix in touch-XY terms — but only if your 11.5/5 mm numbers hold up under independent measurement, because the literature uses different units (counts, static offset) and different tasks (taps, not rests). Do not claim "drift correction" generally; claim the narrow resting-drift operating point if you can defend the rig.

### Mode 2 — ENSLAVEMENT / finger coupling: phenomenon old, application apparently new
- KNOWN, thoroughly: finger enslavement/interdependence in FORCE and free MOTION (neighbour ~67% force share; >60% angular enslavement adjacent; index independent ~61% ROM / 0.81 index vs 0.48 ring; EE ~20% of total MVC by NN; dynamic increments 17–24%). Regression/NN have even been compared FOR FORCE-enslavement matrices (NN judged better for force deficit). No dispute: the biology is not yours.
- NOT FOUND: any touch-UI paper/patent reporting 62–92% parallel motion of neighbouring TOUCH POINTS on a capacitive surface, or using PER-PAIR LINEAR REGRESSION on simultaneous touch XY to suppress that coupling with stated suppression. Touch-UI neighbours solve different problems: hardware ghosts (mutual capacitance), who-is-touching (DiamondTouch chairs / capacitive user ID), which-finger identity (SpeciFingers / reference-point likelihood), static offset (Holz, Weir GPR), electrical two-finger impedance (Vardar). None is a motion-decoupler.
- Bottom line: you did NOT discover enslavement. You APPEAR to be first to (a) quantify it as touch-point covariation (62–92%) in a resting/multi-touch UI context and (b) treat it with per-pair linear regression. That combination was not found. It is narrow but defensible as novel — provided you distinguish force/motion enslavement (known) from touch-signal decoupling (your claim) and publish the regression details + suppression numbers, otherwise it reads as relabelling known biomechanics.
